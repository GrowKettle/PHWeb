Summary: Let the Calendar Build Itself

During Session 1 on 6/23/26, we proposed a powerful idea, a calendar isn’t just a planning tool, it’s a promise that builds trust.

In Session 2, we dove into how to build a unit calendar.

The Shift

It starts with changing your perspective from ‘What should we do? to What fits here? Rather than trying to create everything at once, you work within something that already has structure.

The Anchor Calendar

The structure of your unit calendar starts by identifying the ‘anchor events’ found on school, community, Kettle Country, and Bay-Lakes calendars. Add in your unit’s must-do traditions. You now have the fixed points that your program has to operate around. They’re no longer conflicts or surprises. See school calendars and anchor calendar template. Anchors to Framework

The year is no longer empty. You have a framework that consolidates the anchor events. Most units have more good ideas than they have time for. Your task becomes placement among the anchors, not selecting activities. Put your unit activities among the anchors and you’ve got a start on a calendar. The calendar isn’t perfect but it’s intentional.

Four Things

Before Session 3 on 7/21/26, consider doing four things…

  1. Gather the school calendar that affects your families.
  2. Add the major Council and District events.
  3. Identify key community events or recurring conflicts.
  4. Then begin placing the major activities that are important to your unit.

Once the framework is in place, everything else becomes easier. Clarity beats completeness.
