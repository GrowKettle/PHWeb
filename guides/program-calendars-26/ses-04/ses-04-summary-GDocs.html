Building Strong Units Through Program Calendars Webinar Series

Session 4, 8/4/26
Summary

Session 4 focused on the experience of a connected program. We talked about connecting the unit’s activities to each other. When activities connect, the unit’s program changes from static to dynamic. It moves. It becomes a journey toward a goal.

Families are more likely to stay engaged when they experience a connected program. They understand that their child is moving toward the larger goal of becoming a responsible adult.

Leaders of units with connected programs aren’t stressed by constantly starting over. They perceive the path on which activities happen. Each activity has three parts: preparation, execution, and reflection. The reflection after one activity flows into the preparation for the next. They see activities as Lego blocks that fit together into the structure of the unit’s larger goal.

Scouting isn’t intended to be a series of disconnected activities. It’s intended to be an experience that guides young people as they grow.

In Session 1, we gave you models and templates to help you. In Session 2, we showed you how to use fixed events like school, district, and council events to anchor your calendar. And in Session 3, we talked about the importance of building trust through predictability.

Now you’re ready to build your unit’s program calendar in earnest. First, though, consider how a single major activity ties to what came before and prepares for what comes after using the Program Connection Map. Answer three questions…

  • What prepares Scouts and families for the experience?
  • What should happen during the experience?
  • What should this experience make possible next?

 Then look for connections among activities as you build your unit calendar using the models and templates from Session 1.
